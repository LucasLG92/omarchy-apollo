# r5 — A desktop that boots on its own

Apollo Linux now has a verified native installation on the Xiaomi Mi 10T Pro: Arch Linux ARM, KDE Plasma Wayland and the Adreno 650 running from the phone’s internal storage. The owner powered the phone on without a cable, reached the desktop and reconnected it to the PC. A new boot, persistent data, installed kernel and stored boot image were checked afterwards.

This milestone moves the project from repeated bring-up work toward a development environment you can keep on the device. It remains experimental: the next step is broader hardware support and sustained use, not a claim that every phone function is finished.

## The engineering behind the milestone

**A clock fix with a reproducible failure.** Kernel 23 avoids powering an unprepared orphan clock’s new parent during topology adoption. The failure was reproduced in the real clock framework: two new tests failed before the patch; all three new cases pass afterwards. The enabled clock test suite reports 117 passes and four existing skips. On the phone, the observed DSI PLL failure and two clock reference warnings disappear; the display continues at 1080×2400 / 144 Hz. Two SPMI warnings remain.

**USB maintenance independent of initramfs timing.** Setup now belongs to a systemd service after the root switch. It adopts a compatible connection without rewriting it and responds when the controller appears later. Native cold boot, physical cable reconnection and recovery from a short RTC suspend were checked, alongside 12 simulated USB cases.

**Hardware regressions checked on the installed system.** Four cameras delivered 90 frames each before and after suspend and again after installation. Focus controls passed 100 cycles before and 40 after; the front sensor passed 24 additional cycles after suspend. Vulkan/Wayland rendering passed on each side of suspend and after installation. Power-cycle fixtures temporarily paused and restored camera observers; they do not prove optical autofocus or battery life.

**A recoverable upgrade path for this transition.** The 22→23 updater checks model, artifacts, native trial evidence, battery and boot geometry; preserves a full private boot-partition backup; and verifies the stored image and installed package. Seven owned-file tests cover rollback and invalid inputs. The actual update preserved userdata. The tests do not guarantee recovery from electrical power loss during a write.

## What comes next

- Fingerprint enrollment, matching and unlock; contact detection alone is already documented on kernel 21.
- Full audio and microphone routes, optical camera behavior and longer power/suspend tests.
- Calls, SMS and mobile data with an active SIM, plus Bluetooth/NFC/OTG accessories.
- Investigation of external display support. The direct USB-C monitor test still produces no image; no working video output is claimed.

## Release scope

**Development prerelease, sources only.** Installed revision: `apollo-linux-test-2026.10.29-r5`; package: `linux-apollo 7.1.0-23`; target: `M2007J3SG / Apollo`. This is Arch Linux ARM, not an official CachyOS release or a universal OS installer. Images, proprietary firmware, calibration data, backups and private logs are not included. A clean end-to-end image build remains to be validated.
